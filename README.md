# Household app: source code

This `source` branch holds the code. The `main` branch holds the built site that GitHub Pages serves at https://thewylander.github.io/household/.

## Make a change

1. Edit `src/`: `app.js` for screens and behaviour, `logic.js` for money maths and CSV parsing, `styles.css` for the look.
2. Build with `python3 build.py` (Python with Pillow; it fetches the Firebase SDK from npm the first time). The site lands in `pwa/site/`.
3. Test with `node test/logic.test.js`, `node test/deals_job.test.mjs`, `node test/notify.test.mjs`, `python3 test/e2e_pwa.py`, `python3 test/bounds.py`, `python3 test/motion.py`, `python3 test/push.py` and `python3 test/receipt.py` (Playwright and Chrome). Bounds checks every screen at 320px and 390px wide; motion checks entrances, count-ups and swipes. The other tests run with reduced motion so they read final values.
4. Deploy: copy everything in `pwa/site/` to the root of `main`, keeping `README.md` and `.nojekyll`. Also copy `src/github/deals.mjs` to `.github/deals/deals.mjs`, `src/github/deals.yml` to `.github/workflows/deals.yml`, `src/github/notify.mjs` and `src/logic.js` to `.github/notify/`, and `src/github/notify.yml` to `.github/workflows/notify.yml`. Then commit and push. Pages updates in about a minute. Close the app fully and reopen it to get the new version.
5. Add a draft note at the top of `RELEASES` in `src/app.js` for anything worth noticing. Whoever set the app up reviews it in Settings, Notes for the other person, edits it and decides when it's shown. The other person sees it once.

## Keep out of this repo (it's public)

- `household-preset.json`: names, card and rent for the private Claude version.
- `pwa-config.json`: the two sign-in emails, only needed to rebuild the Firestore rules file.
- Any Splitwise or bank CSV.

`.gitignore` already covers these. Two more browser test suites use them, so they aren't here.

## Deals

The Deals tab reads `deals.json` from the `deals` branch. A GitHub Actions job (`.github/workflows/deals.yml` on `main`) rebuilds it every morning (Wednesday's new specials, Umall's Saturday picks, and anything marked down in between), for free, publishing only when something is new, from public sources: OzBargain's grocery RSS feeds (plus its Liquorland feed, as Liquorland's own site turns GitHub away), the weekly Catalogue Half-Price Report posted on OzBargain (every Coles and Woolworths half-price item in the NSW catalogue), Harris Farm's online specials, and Umall's (an online Asian grocer, a Shopify store) public collection feeds: half-price, Saturday to Tuesday, and weekly specials, Wednesday to Friday. Meat Emporium (Sydney's big meat market, also Shopify) is read from its full product list on every run: anything with a was-price above its price and in stock is a special, with a photo, running to the end of the specials week (its sales have no end day, so they're looked at again each run). United Meat Co has no product feed (its specials are posted on social media), so it's only recognised as a shop when OzBargain posts one of its deals. Umall items show only until their run ends. Harris Farm and Umall items carry the product photo from their Shopify stores (only from Shopify's image CDN, asked for at the size shown); Coles and Woolies have none, as the half-price list has no product codes. Next week's half-price list is often posted days early, so it waits while this week's is still running; with nothing running, it shows with the day it starts. It needs no secrets. The file holds the same store specials for everyone; each phone filters it against its own watch list, which stays in Firebase. Run it by hand from the Actions tab with "Run workflow". Harris Farm's "was" prices are often per kilo, so its specials show at the special price only.

The tab puts everything on special first, with a search, two drop-downs (shop, and category, which includes the watch list) and a sort button (best for us, biggest saving, biggest % off, lowest price, A to Z), and "Rarely this cheap" (items the half-price list says haven't been half price for 8 weeks or more). Below that: the best deal for each word on the watch list (edited in a panel), and OzBargain posts for our shops, most votes first. Sale days live in the Calendar: marked on the month (a filled dot when we're keen), listed under it with a Keen button each, and on each day's card, where you can add one for that day. Home shows the next sale day, and its link and the sale-day pop-ups open the Calendar on that day. Each special, and each watch-list match, has a + that adds it to the weekend shopping list or puts it on the watch list (with a starting word, minus the size). The list opens from the trolley at the top of Deals: grouped by shop, with each special's price and when it ends, tick boxes, your own items, and a total at the special prices. Each item still to get has Check price buttons: a Coles and a Woolies search for it (the name without price talk, keeping brand and size), opened in the shop's own site, so nothing is scraped and no data is stored. It's stored with the watch list in `hh/deals` (`list`), so both phones share it.

### Specials from screenshots

Coles and Liquorland don't let other sites read their specials (their sites turn automated readers away), so the full Coles specials, fresh food included, and Liquorland's come in by hand: "Add specials from screenshots", under Everything on special, takes up to 12 screenshots of a shop's specials page, or a full-page PDF saved from Safari. Each goes to Gemini in turn, with the same setup as receipts, and comes back as each product's name, special price, usual price, any deal words ("2 for $10") and aisle. You check them in a tick list, with the shop and the last day of the specials (the one shown, else the Tuesday the specials week ends), and they're saved in `hh/deals` (`snaps`), so both phones see them. They show in the list marked "From a screenshot", count for the watch list and its pop-ups, and go after their last day. One the half-price list already has isn't doubled up, the same product added again replaces the one before, and a wrong one can be taken off from its panel. Meat and seafood has its own aisle in the category drop-down.

## Receipts

"Capture a receipt" (the + opens a choice of Capture a receipt, which goes straight to the camera, or Add a spend; it is also in Upload) sends the photo to Gemini through Firebase AI Logic, using the Gemini Developer API's free tier, which needs the Firebase project to stay on the no-cost Spark plan. Gemini returns the shop, date, total, a category and each item as JSON; you check it, tap anything that's just one person's, and it saves the spends. Things that are just the payer's are left out; things that are just the other person's are saved as a spend they owe in full. The photo isn't stored. The AI module (`firebase-ai.js`), the App Check module (`firebase-app-check.js`) and the modular app module are copied from the Firebase npm package at build time and load only when a receipt is snapped. Firebase AI Logic has required App Check since 2 November 2026, so the app proves each request comes from it with an invisible reCAPTCHA Enterprise check and a fresh one-use token per request (this works whether replay protection is on or off). One-time setup: (1) Google Cloud console, reCAPTCHA, Create key: type Web, your Pages domain, "Use checkbox challenge" off. (2) Firebase console, Security, App Check, Apps: register the web app with reCAPTCHA Enterprise and that key. (3) Firebase console, AI Logic, Get started, Gemini Developer API; this also enforces App Check for AI Logic (check under App Check, APIs). (4) Put the key ID in `pwa-config.json` as `appCheckKey` and build; it is public, like the rest of the web config. iPhones block reCAPTCHA in home screen web apps (it fails with `appCheck/recaptcha-error`), so the app can use an App Check debug token instead: make one in the Firebase console (App Check, Apps, the web app's menu, Manage debug tokens) and paste it into Upload, Receipt reading setup. It is stored in `hh/ai`, which only the two signed-in users can read, never in this repo; with it saved, App Check skips reCAPTCHA. Google intends debug tokens for testing, so delete and replace it if it ever leaks. Without a billing account on the project, reCAPTCHA stays on its free tier of 10,000 checks a month. The model names are in `AI_MODELS` in `src/app.js`; if the first isn't available, the next one is tried.

## Phone notifications

Each phone turns them on in Settings (on iPhone, from the Home Screen app, iOS 16.4 or later). Every pop-up's bold title says what it's about (a new spend, a sale day, the week's top specials), with the details under it. The iPhone adds "from Two of Us" itself, so the app's name isn't repeated; `APP` in `notify.mjs` only stands in for a pop-up with no title. The first phone makes the Web Push key pair and stores it with the subscriptions in `hh/push`. The `Phone notifications` workflow (`.github/workflows/notify.yml`) runs each morning and evening, and straight after each deals refresh (`workflow_run`): the evening before and the morning of a sale day marked Keen, the week's top specials once that week's lists have started (one pop-up, by Friday: watch-list matches first, then items not half price for two months or more, longest first, then the biggest savings, three lines under the heading), new watch-list matches after that (Umall's on Saturday too), and any new spends that are still waiting.

GitHub's scheduled runs can start hours late (in November 2026 they ran 6 to 10 hours after their times, and the Wednesday refresh didn't start at all before 9am), so:

- A run between 9:30pm and 6:30am Sydney time (`AWAKE` in `notify.mjs`) sends nothing and leaves what's due for the next run. New spends started from the app still go straight away.
- When a list shown in Deals has run out (Harris Farm, Meat Emporium and the half-price list on Tuesdays, Umall on Tuesdays and Fridays), the app starts the deals workflow itself with the saved GitHub token, at most every two hours from each phone, says which lists it's getting, and reads the new file from GitHub's contents API (fresh within a minute; the raw link is cached for five) for the next eight minutes. The refresh then runs the notifications workflow, so a late Wednesday pop-up goes out as soon as one of us opens the app.
- For pop-ups on the dot, an outside timer (cron-job.org, say) can start the deals workflow at 7:35am and 7:35pm Sydney time with `POST /repos/OWNER/household/actions/workflows/deals.yml/dispatches` and `{"ref":"main"}`, using its own fine-grained token (this repo, Actions: Read and write). The token stays with the timer, never here.

New spends pop up on the other person's phones. The phone that adds a spend (by hand, from a receipt, or by closing a day tab) notes it in `hh/push` under `events.a` or `events.b`: the total, what it was, who paid and the other person's share, written for their lock screen, keeping the last 20 from the past three days. Each person can turn these off in Settings. If a GitHub token is saved in `hh/ai`, the phone then starts the workflow straight away (`workflow_dispatch` with `mode: spends`, which sends only new spends and welcomes), so the pop-up lands within a minute; without one, the next morning or evening run sends them, several at once as one pop-up. The token is a fine-grained personal access token for this repo only, with Actions: Read and write, made from the link in Settings (Phone notifications, Set up instant pop-ups), which fills in everything but the repo. The app checks it by starting the job, with a test pop-up for the phone that saved it, and only keeps it if GitHub says yes. The link asks for 364 days (GitHub won't take a year or more), or pick No expiration on the page; when GitHub turns it down, Settings says why, and a new one takes two minutes. It never goes in this repo.

It signs in to Firebase as its own bot user through Firebase's REST APIs, so it needs no Google Cloud service account key (Google Workspace organisations block those by default). One-time setup:

1. Firebase console, Authentication, Users, Add user: the bot email and a long random password. The bot email is the first sign-in email with `+bot` before the @, or `botEmail` in `pwa-config.json`.
2. Publish the rules that `python3 build.py` writes to `pwa/firestore.rules`. Their `isBot()` lets that user read `hh/deals` and `hh/push`, and change only `subs`, `sent` and `last` in `hh/push`. New spends need no change to these rules.
3. Add two repository secrets, `BOT_EMAIL` and `BOT_PASSWORD`.

It does nothing until both secrets exist. It also runs once whenever its code changes on `main`. Its run log is public, so each run's note says how many pop-ups went out and what kind, never what they said. The list of runs shows when the app started one for a new spend, but nothing about the spend.

## Loading screen

While the app opens, it shows the other person's photo rotating (a CSS `rotate` loop, 2.2s a turn, that stands still with reduced motion). The picture fits inside a circle its box can hold, so it never pokes out as it goes round, and the drop shadow sits on the box so it stays underneath. Each phone keeps that picture in localStorage (`hh.splash`), keyed to a fingerprint of the photo and cleared on signing out, since the loading screen shows before sign-in. It cuts the person out on the phone itself with MediaPipe's selfie segmenter (`@mediapipe/tasks-vision` 1.2.2 from jsDelivr, about 15MB, fetched only when a new photo needs a cut-out), so the photo never leaves the phone. If that fails, or there's nobody in the photo, the round photo turns instead. Tests swap the segmenter for `window.HH_CUTOUT`.

## Spends left out of the budget

A spend can be kept out of the monthly budget with the Counts towards our monthly budget box (entry field `offBudget`), on adding, editing, closing a day tab or checking a receipt. It still counts in the split and the balance, is marked Not in budget where it's listed, and is left out of every budget number alongside categories left out in Customise (`L.inBudget`, `L.budgetTotals`). Where a month's spend sits next to the budget, the app adds a note such as + $120.00 not in the budget.

## Welcome

The first time the other person signs in, they land on a welcome page: a five-item tour of what they can do (add a spend, stay square, grab the specials, every day and sale days, pop-ups), with Come on in at the bottom. The person who set the app up can add a note in Settings (Customise the welcome), which then shows at the end of the tour; with no note there's just the tour. Coming in marks the latest update note as read, so the tour isn't followed by an update dialog. It only shows to someone who hasn't been in yet.

## Where the data lives

Firebase (Firestore and email sign-in). The Firestore rules are set in the Firebase console and only let the two verified emails in, plus the notification bot, which can read sale days, the watch list, phones and new-spend notes. `config/firebase.json` is the web config the live site already serves, so it isn't secret.
